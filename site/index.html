A living world that runs on your own machine — and doesn't wait for you.

    You answer a short interview and a world is forged around you: a place, and people with interiors, schedules, and things they are not saying.

    • A short interview

      Who you are, what you want, how big a place you want to live in. The forge writes the rest — the town, the cast, the friendships and the grudges, the shifts, the secrets. Answer every question, or answer none of them and let it surprise you.

    • Its own clock

      The world runs whether or not you are there. Shifts get covered, nights out happen, two of them talk about you. Come back after a week and a week has gone by.

    • You text them

      No parser, no commands. The people in your world sit in a list of conversations like anyone else in your phone — and they text you first, because something happened, or because it has been three days and one of them noticed.

    • Walls, not hope

      Who knows what is typed and enforced. A secret is held by the people who were actually told it, and nobody accidentally knows something they never heard — not because the model remembered, but because it was never handed the sentence.

    • Your hardware, or your key

      It runs against a local model on your own machine, with no cloud dependency at runtime and nothing to subscribe to. Or bring an API key and point it at something larger. The world is the same world either way.

    • A file you own

      Your world is a JSON file and a database on your disk. Readable, editable, copyable, backed up like anything else you keep.

    And then a story

    A story can be laid over a living world.

    A mystery, a debt, a disappearance. It does not replace the world and it does not consume it. It sits on top of the people who are already there and points what they know — and what they are keeping — at one question.

    It has a real shape. It builds. Then it goes deliberately quiet — the stretch every generated story gets wrong, by either escalating forever or going slack. Then it breaks, and someone who was never lying to you, who believed it and said so every time you asked, turns out to have been wrong.

    And it resolves. The murder is solved. The thing is found. The name gets said out loud to the right person.

    Then the story is over and the world keeps going — the same people, the same shifts, the same diner that closes at two, who simply no longer have that question between them. And you can lay another one on.

    What it needs

    Less than you'd think.

    A computer you own — Linux, macOS, or Windows. The engine is PHP 8.1 or newer with the usual extensions (sqlite3, pdo_sqlite, mbstring, curl); the launcher checks all of it on first run and names anything missing by name. A world on disk is a JSON file and a SQLite database — megabytes, not gigabytes.

    A model, from either direction. Local: anything that speaks the OpenAI chat API — llama.cpp, Ollama, LM Studio — found automatically on the ports those actually use. Around 12B is the floor for forging a world; once it exists, the runtime is comfortable on smaller models, because the prompts are engineered to cache. Or bring an API key instead and skip local models entirely — a key is used for the request that carries it and is never stored.

    Running that local model is more ordinary than its reputation: a 12B quantized to four bits is a one-time download of roughly seven gigabytes and wants about eight gigabytes of video memory to answer quickly — one mid-range consumer graphics card, the kind sold for gaming. The smaller models the runtime is happy on fit in half that. No graphics card at all still works through the same servers on CPU and ordinary RAM, just slower — a forge becomes a coffee's worth of waiting instead of a few minutes. The engine itself is the light part; the model is the only thing in the house doing heavy lifting.

    Nothing else. No account, no cloud dependency at runtime, nothing phones home. Connecting a model is a choice you make on a screen built for it — the app finds what is running, and nothing runs on it until you say so.
